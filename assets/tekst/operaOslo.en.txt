🎭 Oslo Opera House (Operahuset Oslo) – An Architectural and Cultural Landmark
📌 Quick overview
Opened: 12 April 2008
Architect: Snøhetta (Norwegian architecture firm)
Style: Contemporary architecture built from white marble and glass, designed to look like a floating block of ice.
Location: On the Oslofjord waterfront in the city center, close to the railway station and the harbor.
Capacity: 1,364 seats for opera and ballet performances.
Symbolism: The largest cultural building in Norway and a symbol of modern Norwegian culture.

💡 Why is it unique?

It is the first opera house in the world with a roof you can walk on, allowing visitors to enjoy panoramic views of the Oslofjord and the city.
It is an eco-friendly building, made with sustainable materials and energy-saving systems.
It presents an international repertoire of operas, ballets, and concerts from around the world.

🎤 Famous performers
Opera singers: Anna Netrebko, Plácido Domingo, Jonas Kaufmann.
Ballet companies: Norwegian National Ballet, Royal Ballet (London).
Orchestras: Oslo Philharmonic Orchestra, Bergen Philharmonic Orchestra.

🏛️ Architecture and design
Facade: 15,500 square meters of white marble from Italy and Greece.
Roof: Walking on it is free and it offers panoramic views of the Oslofjord and the city.
Interior: Wooden walls and ceilings with acoustics optimized for musical performances.
Sculptures and installations: Includes works by well-known Norwegian and international artists.

📸 Best photo spots

The panoramic roof – views of the Oslofjord and the harbor.
The main entrance facade – huge glass walls reflecting the water.
The indoor amphitheater – wooden walls and ceilings.

🎟️ Tickets and visits
🎟️ How to buy tickets?
Online: The official Oslo Opera House website (in English and Norwegian).
On site: The opera box office (open from 10:00 to 18:00).
Price levels:
Front row: 1000–1200 NOK
Middle row: 500–800 NOK
Back row: 200–400 NOK
Students/seniors: 20% discount.

🚪 Free visit
Guided tours: Free (includes a walk through the building and information about its architecture).
Languages: Norwegian, English, German, French.
Duration: 1 hour (usually at 12:00 and 15:00).
Reservation: Not required, but recommended for groups.
